I then picked **Ryan Reynolds** (`ryan-reynolds@sha256:bc12246d…9404`), the nearest dry, self-deprecating voice. The ask: "Use Silvertongue to write a happy-birthday message to a friend, in English, as Ryan Reynolds." Packet settings: format `post`, length `short`, platform `whatsapp`, intensity and edge `full`, emoji `on`. The voice itself says no emoji. The packet was fetched at 2026-11-11 05:40:57 UTC.
- **Draft, exactly as written:**

  > Happy birthday to my oldest friend. And I don't mean how long we've known each other. You're just genuinely very old now.

  Receipt: https://runx.ai/r/669eda206980fd632b52ce767641835f82690c1d76a4812dd297b46de7ed1611
- **Does it sound like him: 7/10.** It uses his "literal twist" move: a sweet opening line, then the meaning flips flatly in the last clause. Affection comes out as an insult, it is short, and it has no emoji or exclamation marks, as the packet asks. It loses points because the joke is a familiar "you're old" gag. His best lines add a strange physical image, the way the packet's "bowls got engaged" example does.
- **One thing that confused me or didn't work:** Searching for a missing voice by a related term gives noisy results. `search_voices {"query":"Iron Man"}` returned 14 voices that have nothing to do with it, including Jane Austen, Barack Obama and Spock. `{"query":"Tony Stark"}` returned Tony Hinchcliffe. There was no hint that the voice I wanted does not exist. Also, on the first attempt the cloud sandbox's network policy blocked the host (`CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403`) until `mcp.silvertongue.dev` was added to the environment's allowed hosts.
- **One change I would make:** When a name query has no match, have `search_voices` return an explicit `"not_in_library": true` plus 2–3 voices with a similar *style* (for example "deadpan, self-deprecating"). Results that only share a word in the name are less useful.
