## Story
Original fictional workplace story. Target delivery: 45–55 seconds with a natural English narrator. Candidate title: My Boss Said I Overslept. I Showed the Owner His Text. #shorts

My boss texted me a photo of the line outside our bakery. “Why is the door locked?” I was in the parking lot. I had the keys. The day before, I’d clocked in one minute late. He took fifteen minutes off my timesheet. Then he asked me to come in tomorrow at eight forty-five to set up a wedding cake pickup. I asked if I could clock in early. “You’re paid from nine,” he said. “End of discussion.” At nine, I opened the door. He was already telling the owner I’d overslept. So I slid my phone across the counter. I’d texted him at eight forty-five. “I’m here. Can I clock in?” His reply was right there. “No.” The owner looked at him. “Give her the fifteen minutes back. Tomorrow, you open.”

- 3–7 s: match cut to the keys beside the narrator. Then show the parking lot as the scene changes to the previous day.
- Show the timecard deduction and the conflicting instructions with two short, legible graphics.
- At the counter, stage the manager's accusation, the phone being turned toward the owner, and the reply NO. Keep the exact text as an editorial overlay.
- Finish on the owner's line, then a reaction shot of the manager. No outro before the payoff.
- Stylized low-poly animation for reliable characters, props and framing. Use Flow only for a short reference-based test or a shot that needs richer motion. Match the existing character and prop designs.
- Voice: conversational US English, lightly exasperated, a pause before “No” and dry delivery on “Tomorrow, you open.” Use a clean close voice, subtle sound effects, and no bed until dialogue is clear.
