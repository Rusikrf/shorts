## Findings and pilot decisions

1. Length alone does not explain reach. The measured top set includes 15–24-second clips and a 158-second story.
2. Duration should follow the time needed to set up and resolve the conflict. Do not stretch the flight story to a preset runtime.
3. The animated examples establish that animation and high views can coexist. Their existing audiences materially limit comparisons with Storytelling Vibe.
4. MSA's most-viewed item in its returned window is music; Boggs' top three includes a film promotion. Keep such rows visible but exclude them from story-only conclusions.
5. The available MSA story transcript starts with a confrontation, then delays background until 0:16.
6. TheOdd1sOut starts with a concrete object introduced in dialogue, then attaches a consequential wish. Give the flight pilot its own concrete object: the flight map.
7. TheAMaazing opens on a specific contradiction about chili, then answers it conversationally. Give the narrator a genuine question to resolve instead of a generic teaser.
8. Claude's Reddit-heavy sample favors longer complete stories, but background and runtime vary together. Test two visual treatments on identical audio and pacing if comparing visuals.
9. Claude's article-style headline card and one-word captions are hypotheses, not universal requirements. Start with one prominent hook line, then captions that follow speech. Avoid fake vote counts or presenting reconstructed events as verified.
10. Judge the pilot on feed appearances, stayed-to-watch, engaged views and retention near the discovery and ending. Compare at the same age after posting; do not infer why people stayed from public view totals.

## First pilot

Consider opening on the map discovery, then rewind to the airport, if consistent with the verified story outline. Keep the explanation of what was checked short and concrete, and resolve what the narrator did next. Put the key information in the visuals: the route, call, uncertainty, and handoff. Coordinate voice and timing before animating the route sequence.
