# P03 — Clock rule: production pack (ChatGPT)

Status: SCRIPT + STORYBOARD READY (not a rendered video)

## Premise and source

A hospital IT help desk manager sent a written policy requiring agents to be available at 06:59:59 and still on calls until 16:00:01. A staff member complied literally on a last-minute call. [Original Reddit post](https://www.reddit.com/r/MaliciousCompliance/comments/1ubd7he/just_complying_with_your_written_expectations_and/), posted 2026-06-21. The user reports the event; its truth is not independently verified. **Do not invent firing, promotion, lawsuit, or policy reversal.**

## Script

01. My manager said I had to stay on calls until four-oh-one seconds past four. So I hung up at that exact moment.
02. I worked on an IT help desk at a hospital.
03. Every shift started with logging into seven different programs. That took ten to fifteen minutes.
04. Then the manager sent us an email.
05. We had to be ready for calls one second before the shift started, and stay on the phone until one second after it ended.
06. So I followed that email. For a while.
07. Then a call came in with less than two minutes left on my shift.
08. I answered, took the caller's name and location, and listened as they began describing the problem.
09. The clock hit four p.m. and one second.
10. I apologized, said my shift was over, ended the call, logged off, and went home.
11. The caller complained. My manager called me into his office.
12. He said I should have finished the call.
13. I showed him the email he'd sent us.
14. It said I could log off one second after my shift ended.
15. And that was exactly what I had done.
16. Did it help my career? Probably not. But it was his rule.
